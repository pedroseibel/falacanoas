# Design brief: the Fala digest PDF (resumo diário to the Ouvidoria)

Research brief, 2026-10-13. Sources marked [secundário] were reached only through a secondary page; [não verificado] means not confirmed from a primary source. pdfa.org (PDF/UA in a Nutshell, the Matterhorn PDF, "Conforming to both") and the UMich Tufte notes returned 403, so the PDF/A and PDF/UA pairing claims rest on search snippets.

## 1. Principles for dense information documents

- **Tufte, data-ink and layout**: five rules: show the data, maximize data-ink, erase non-data-ink, erase redundant data-ink, revise [secondário, course notes: https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/7450/13/Notes/tufte.pdf]; "Regardless of its cause, it is all non-data-ink or redundant data-ink, and it is often chartjunk" (VDQI, quoted at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data-ink_ratio).
- **Tufte, tables and boxes**: on sparkline tables, "avoid data frames and heavy boxes"; the alignment of numbers, words and graphics already forms an implicit grid. A word-sized graphic can sit inside a table row. https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/
- **Tufte, comparison and small multiples**: "Compared to what?" is the heart of quantitative reasoning (Envisioning Information p.67, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_multiple [secundário]). Small multiples are repeated designs within the eye span, sharing context and differing in content (https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2005/12/small-multiples-within-a-user-interface.php [secundário]). Envisioning Information's chapters include Micro/Macro Readings and Layering and Separation; I only have course summaries ("readable whole and in parts"; "separate categories, give layers hierarchy") [secondário, não verificado against the book]. "Smallest effective difference" is Visual Explanations p.73, not Envisioning Information (https://boxesandarrows.com/three-lessons-from-tufte-special-deliverable-6 [secundário]).
- **Butterick, measure**: 45–90 characters per line, spaces included; two to three alphabets per line. https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html
- **Bringhurst, measure**: 45–75 satisfactory, 66 ideal, 40–50 for multiple columns (webtypography.net, quoting the book: https://webtypography.net/2.1.2 [secundário]).
- **Butterick, margins and size**: default 1" margins are too small for proportional fonts; bottom margin about 0.25" larger than top (https://practicaltypography.com/page-margins.html). Print body 10–12 pt (https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html); line spacing 120–145% (https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html); set "keep with next" on headings (https://practicaltypography.com/keep-with-next-paragraph.html). Butterick has no headers/footers page; use WCAG PDF14 and the Manual instead.
- **Butterick, tables**: turn borders off, add back only where needed; slightly larger cell padding is the main readability fix (https://practicaltypography.com/tables.html). Rules half a point to one point, solid, never thick; prefer space to a rule (https://practicaltypography.com/rules-and-borders.html). Tabular figures are "essential for … vertically aligned columns"; test with a line of zeroes over ones (https://practicaltypography.com/alternate-figures.html). Digits of the same meaning must align; identifiers are not magnitudes (https://practicaltypography.com/grids-of-numbers.html).
- **Schwabish, "Ten Guidelines for Better Tables"** (J. Benefit-Cost Analysis 11(2), 2020; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-benefit-cost-analysis/article/abs/ten-guidelines-for-better-tables/74C6FD9FEB12038A52A95B9FBCA05A12): offset heads from body; right-align numbers, left-align text; sensible decimals; units only once; highlight outliers; subtle dividers; white space between rows and groups; charts for very large tables. List as summarised at https://www.r-bloggers.com/2020/09/10-guidelines-to-better-table-design/ [secundário].
- **UK Analysis Function (government statistics)**: right-align figures and their headings; same precision per column; thousands separators; leading zero; "do not over use gridlines"; "shading should be avoided … whenever possible", and 3:1 contrast if used; no blank rows or columns; headline titles stating the key message; source beneath; sans, 12 pt or larger in text documents. https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-tables/
- **GOV.UK summary list**: key/value pairs for a record's facts; use a table for tabular data; row borders help zoom/magnifier users. https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/summary-list/
- **Information Mapping** (chunks under labels; Horn): only background found [secundário, não verificado]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_writing

## 2. Structure patterns for briefing and operational documents

- **BLUF**: open with the main point; AR 25-50's standard is "concise, organized, and to the point" with "bottom line up front" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLUF_(communication) [secundário]). The current 2020 AR 25-50 text was not checked, and one source attributes the phrase to a rescinded DA pamphlet [não verificado]. The inverted pyramid has the same logic: key facts first, supporting detail after, so readers who stop early still get the point (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inverted-pyramid/).
- **Question-style headings in Brazilian public-sector summaries**: TCU one-page "Resumo": O QUE FOI REALIZADO? / O QUE O TCU ENCONTROU? / O QUE O TCU DECIDIU? / QUAIS SÃO OS BENEFÍCIOS ESPERADOS?, then a "DADOS DA DELIBERAÇÃO" reference box (Acórdão, date, relator, TC, unit). Tagged pt-BR. https://portal.tcu.gov.br/data/files/58/56/05/BA/E1A9E8103A4A64C8F18818A8/Auditoria%20do%20TCU%20sobre%20as%20contratacoes%20de%20obras%20de%20reconstrucao%20do%20Museu%20Nacional.pdf
- **Ouvidoria's own reporting vocabulary**: Lei 13.460/2017 art. 15 says the management report states counts of manifestações, their reasons, analysis of recurring points, and measures taken. Art. 16 gives the ouvidoria 30 days (extendable once, with justification) to send the final decision to the user, and 20 days (same extension) for agents to answer information requests. The 30 days is the Ouvidoria's duty to the resident; Fala's public clock is Fala's own convention, so don't present it as the statutory deadline. https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2017/lei/l13460.htm
- **Repeating identity on every page**: FEMA ICS-201 repeats Incident Name, Number and Date/Time Initiated on each page, then map/sketch, situation summary, "Prepared by" block, time-stamped action log, resource table. https://training.fema.gov/EMIWeb/IS/ICSResource/assets/ICS%20Forms//ICS%20Form%20201,%20Incident%20Briefing%20(v3).pdf
- **Running heads and page labels**: WCAG PDF14 (running headers and footers with title, chapter, page number) and PDF17 (consistent page numbering so viewer and printed numbers match). https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF14 and https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF17
- **Page break per recipient so pages can be split and forwarded**: no source found. It is a design inference from ICS-201 (self-identifying pages) and PDF14. [não verificado]
- **Open311 field checklist** for a service request: id, status, service name/code, description (plain text), agency_responsible, requested/updated/expected datetime, address, lat, long, media_url. https://wiki.open311.org/GeoReport_v2/

## 3. Maps in printed reports

- **Hierarchy (Ordnance Survey)**: reader sees the main map first, then title, locator map or graphics, legend; source and copyright last and smallest. A locator map is smaller scale and simpler. An inset enlargement should be redrawn for its larger scale, with its area marked on the main map by a bounding box. Scale bar low in the hierarchy; north arrow small and simple, only if needed. Title states subject, place and date; no "A map of…". https://docs.os.uk/more-than-maps/geographic-data-visualisation/guide-to-cartography/map-layout
- **Axis Maps**: make visual hierarchy match intellectual hierarchy; contrast drives figure-ground; use the squint test. https://www.axismaps.com/guide/visual-hierarchy. Labels no smaller than about 6–7 pt in print; point-label positions in order above-right, below-right, above-left, below-left; demote labels by at least two points between ranks. The page says nothing on halos or collision handling. https://www.axismaps.com/guide/labeling
- **Numbered markers keyed to a list**: ArcGIS practice is a unique number field labelled on the point, with a table beside the legend as the key (https://support.esri.com/en-us/knowledge-base/how-to-create-numbered-symbols-on-a-map-to-represent-po-000015202). A surveying forum reports that ID numbers on points with a separate table avoid "a flurry of leader arrows" (https://rpls.com/forums/strictly-surveying/example-of-map-of-survey/). Datawrapper: use a map key for what does not fit on the map (https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/locator-maps/). No authoritative cartographic text found on overlap or offset rules [não verificado]. Brewer's small-map guidance also not verified.
- **Colour for print**: ColorBrewer filters for "print friendly" and "photocopy safe". https://colorbrewer2.org/
- **OSM attribution in print**: credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors" plus the ODbL, printing the URL openstreetmap.org/copyright (print can't link); one credit suffices for several static images in a document. https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines
- **OSM tile policy**: bulk or pre-emptive tile fetching is prohibited and the policy doesn't address static-map generation. Treat nightly rendering as bulk; use your own tiles or a vector basemap. https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/

## 4. Brazilian official-document conventions

Source: Manual de Redação da Presidência da República, 3ª ed. 2018 (official page https://www.gov.br/planalto/pt-br/assuntos/manual-de-redacao/manual-de-redacao-da-presidencia-da-republica; the official PDF returned 403 to curl, so I read the same edition from a university mirror, files.ufgd.edu.br). These are for ofícios; echo them, don't claim to be one.

- **Identification (5.1.2)**: document type in capitals, "Nº", number/year/unit codes, left-aligned: `OFÍCIO Nº 652/2018/SAA/SE/MT`. **Date (5.1.3)**: `Brasília, 2 de fevereiro de 2018.`, right-aligned, no leading zero, lowercase month, "1º" for the first. **Assunto (5.1.5)**: bold, "Assunto:" then a short phrase, no verbs, four or five words, full stop.
- **Text (5.1.6)**: direct verbs ("Informo", "Encaminho"), no "Tenho a honra de". Calibri or Carlito 12 pt, single spacing, 6 pt after paragraphs, notes 10 pt. Paragraphs numbered from the first when there are three or more. Forwarding documents state the origin, date and reason.
- **Page (5.1.9, 5.2)**: A4; margins 3 cm left, 1.5 cm right, 2 cm top and bottom; 5 cm header area on page 1; page numbers centred in the footer from page 2; black on white, colour only for graphics; bold for emphasis, avoid italics, underline, caps, shading, borders; signature should not sit alone on a page; closing "Atenciosamente,".
- **File name (5.2.l)**: type + number + year + keywords, e.g. `Ofício 123_2018_relatório produtividade anual`.
- **E-mail (6.4)**: specific subject (6.4.3.1); formal register even in e-mail; a message forwarding a file must say what is in it and whether the attachment is indispensable or could go in the body (6.4.4); unsigned e-mail has documentary value only if the recipient accepts it, since ICP-Brasil certification is the legal bar (6.4.2, MP 2.200-2/2001).
- **Electronic verification footer**: SEI-style documents print a "código verificador" (existence) and "código CRC" (authenticity/version) with a check URL (https://www.tre-sp.jus.br/institucional/sistemas/sei-arquivos/guia-de-conferencia-de-autenticidade-de-documentos; https://www.trf3.jus.br/carta-servicos/sistema-eletronico-de-informacoes-sei-autenticacao-de-documentos). No primary SEI text checked.
- **gov.br Design System (Padrão Digital de Governo)**: table and typography pages did not render in fetches; no document-specific rule confirmed [não verificado]. TCU's "Criando documentos digitais acessíveis" (2021) exists for Word/PowerPoint/PDF but its body wasn't read: https://portal.tcu.gov.br/publicacoes-institucionais/cartilha-manual-ou-tutorial/criando-documentos-digitais-acessiveis

## 5. Accessible and archival PDF

- **e-MAG 3.1 (2014, WCAG 2.0-based), rec. 3.8**: documents preferably in HTML; ODF acceptable; "Se um arquivo for disponibilizado em PDF, deverá ser fornecida uma alternativa em HTML ou ODF." Rec. 3.9–3.10: table caption/summary, TH/TD with THEAD/TBODY/TFOOT. https://www.gov.br/esporte/pt-br/servicos/editais/arquivos/departamento-de-governo-eletronico-manual-de-acessibilidade.pdf/@@download/file. Check whether a newer e-MAG supersedes it [não verificado].
- **GOV.UK**: PDF is "a last resort", always with an HTML version; PDF/A is for archiving and "does not relate to accessibility". If a PDF is made: accessible source, real headings from 1 with no skipped levels, header cells in tables, a meaningful title, correct reading order, descriptive link text, no colour-only meaning, no images of text, alt text or equivalent. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/publishing-accessible-documents. Rationale and the "PDF alongside HTML, never alone" line: https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2018/07/16/why-gov-uk-content-should-be-published-in-html-and-not-pdf/
- **PDF/UA-1 (ISO 14289-1) via the Matterhorn Protocol**: 31 checkpoints, 136 failure conditions; examples include missing alt text, links not nested in Link tags, XMP without a title, headings not tagged as headings, lists not tagged as lists (https://www.pdflib.com/pdf-knowledge-base/pdfua/matterhorn-protocol). Reading order, language, bookmarks and table-header clauses: not confirmed from the standard [não verificado]. PDF/UA-2 (ISO 14289-2, March 2024) is built on PDF 2.0 and doesn't replace UA-1 (https://pdfa.org/iso-14289-2-pdfua-2/ [secundário]).
- **WCAG 2.2 PDF techniques**: PDF1 alt, PDF2 bookmarks, PDF3 tab/reading order, PDF4 artifacts for decoration, PDF6 Table/TR/TH/TD, PDF9 headings, PDF14 running heads, PDF16 /Lang, PDF17 page numbering, PDF18 title, PDF21 lists. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/#pdf. PDF6 doesn't cover repeating header rows across pages.
- **CONARQ OT nº 4 (Oct 2016), PDF/A for archival documents** (https://www.gov.br/conarq/pt-br/assuntos/camaras-tecnicas-setoriais-inativas/camara-tecnica-de-documentos-eletronicos-ctde/Orientacao_tecnica_4.pdf): Rec. 1, "Utilize preferencialmente o formato PDF/A-1" (simplest to generate, validate, view). Rec. 2, use PDF/A-2 if PAdES advanced signature is needed. Rec. 3, don't convert A-1 to A-2/A-3. Rec. 5, check licences of embedded fonts. Rec. 9–12, for A-3 define the allowed attachment profile, refuse "Unspecified" and `application/octet-stream`, scan attachments. Rec. 13, use a conformance validator. Levels: b (visual fidelity), u (+Unicode), a (+tags, language, Unicode, reading order). PDF/A-2 allows transparency, layers, JPEG 2000 and PAdES; A-1 forbids executable JavaScript, external hyperlinks, audio and video. PDF/A is part of e-PING. The e-ARQ Brasil lookup returned nothing on PDF/A parts [não verificado].
- **Conflict to test**: OT 4 says A-1 bans "hyperlinks externos", but a second-hand quote of ISO 19005-1 §6.6.3 implies URI actions are allowed (https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader/hyperlinks-don-t-work-with-reader-9-with-pdf-a/m-p/1617967). Test with veraPDF, and print URLs as visible text anyway.
- **A and UA together**: one file can meet both; the PDF Association guide pairs PDF/A-2 or A-3 (level A) with PDF/UA-1, and A-4 with UA-2 (search snippet only; https://pdfa.org/resource/conforming-to-both-pdf-a-and-pdf-ua-best-practice-guide/ [não verificado]).
- **Tooling that can write it**: WeasyPrint supports `pdf/a-1b…a-4f`, `pdf/ua-1`, tagging (`--pdf-tags`), CSS margin boxes and running elements, but "not guaranteed … valid" (https://doc.courtbouillon.org/weasyprint/stable/api_reference.html). Typst supports A-1a/b, A-2a/b/u, A-3, A-4 and UA-1, writes tagged PDF by default and checks image alt text under UA-1; table header-cell functions are experimental (https://typst.app/docs/reference/pdf/). Chromium/Puppeteer: `tagged` default true, `outline` default false and experimental, `headerTemplate`/`footerTemplate` with pageNumber/totalPages (https://pptr.dev/api/puppeteer.pdfoptions); `@page` margin boxes with `counter(page)` arrived in Chrome 131 (https://developer.chrome.com/blog/print-margins). Chromium emits no PDF/A, so it needs post-processing plus validation [inference]. Repeated header rows: "Print user agents may repeat header rows on each page" (https://www.w3.org/TR/CSS21/tables.html); `break-inside: avoid` keeps a block whole (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/break-inside).

## 7. Recommendations for the Fala digest PDF

1. **Page 1 is the BLUF cover.** Identification block in ofício style (`RESUMO DIÁRIO Nº 0287/2026/FALA-CANOAS`; `Canoas, 13 de outubro de 2026.`; bold `Assunto:` of four to five words, no verbs), one sentence with the day's totals, then a table of recipients with item counts and page numbers. (Manual 5.1.2–5.1.5; BLUF; NN/g inverted pyramid; Lei 13.460 art. 15 for the counts vocabulary.)
2. **One section per recipient, each starting on a new page**, with its own strip (body name, item count, item range). Every page repeats document number, date and recipient in a running head, and "Página X de Y" in the foot. A split-out section stays self-identifying. (ICS-201 repeating header; WCAG PDF14/PDF17; NYC MMR footer; page-per-recipient is an inference.)
3. **Number items continuously across the document**, and print the 5-character code beside the number in a fixed-width or tabular face, left-aligned. "Item 37, código AB3K2" survives phone calls and memorandos. (Manual 5.1.6 numbers paragraphs for citation; Butterick grids-of-numbers on identifiers.)
4. **Each item is a fixed record block repeated identically** (small multiples). Top line: number, code, category, bairro. Then key/value facts (confirmations, sent time, coordinates, URL), the resident text in a column of 45–75 characters, photos as equal-size thumbnails. Avoid enclosing boxes; use space and one hairline between items, with `break-inside: avoid`. (Tufte small multiples and sparkline "no heavy boxes"; Butterick line length; Bringhurst 40–50 for columns; GOV.UK summary list.)
5. **A compact index table opens each section** (no., code, category, bairro, confirmations, page) for the person who protocols item by item. Header offset by a rule, one rule under it, no shading, numbers right-aligned in tabular lining figures, text left-aligned, header row repeated across pages. (Schwabish; Analysis Function; Butterick tables and figures; CSS 2.1 header repetition.)
6. **Typography**: one sans family with open licence and tabular lining figures (Carlito matches the Manual; verify the zeros-over-ones test), body 10–11 pt at 120–145% leading, headings with keep-with-next, bold as the only emphasis, hairline rules of 0.5 pt, black on white so it survives a B/W laser printer. Table and metadata sizes below 10 pt have no source [não verificado]; keep them at 8 pt or more and test on paper. (Butterick; Manual 5.2.h–i; Axis Maps 6–7 pt floor for map labels.)
7. **Maps**: one overview map per section with numbered markers equal to the item numbers (not the codes), keyed to the index table. Cap the markers per map and split by subprefeitura or bairro rather than shrink them. Mark any detail inset with a bounding box on the overview. Small simple north arrow or none, scale bar low in the hierarchy, labels at 6–7 pt or more placed above-right, grayscale-safe symbols. (OS layout guide; Axis Maps; ColorBrewer print-friendly; Esri and Datawrapper for the key.)
8. **Always print coordinates and street address as text** (WGS84, five decimals) next to the map link, so no information lives only in an image. (Open311 fields; FixMyStreet `submit.txt`; GOV.UK "no images of text".)
9. **Attribution once per document**: "Mapa: © OpenStreetMap contributors · openstreetmap.org/copyright" printed in the footer of the map pages. Pre-render from your own tiles or a vector basemap, not by pulling OSM tile servers each night. (OSM attribution guidelines; tile policy.)
10. **Photos**: only moderated photos, at most four thumbnails per item, captioned "Foto 2 de 3", each with alt text; decorative elements as artifacts. (WCAG PDF1/PDF4; GOV.UK.)
11. **Accessibility build**: tagged PDF with Title, `Lang=pt-BR`, H1–H3 with no skipped levels, real Table/TH, Link tags, bookmarks per recipient and per item, correct reading order. Run veraPDF and a PDF/UA checker in CI. (WCAG PDF2/3/6/9/16/18/21; Matterhorn; GOV.UK; e-MAG 3.9–3.10.)
12. **Archival target: PDF/A-2a** (tagged, so also a PDF/UA-1 claim). CONARQ's default is PDF/A-1, but A-2 is required if an ICP-Brasil PAdES signature is ever added, and level "a" carries tags, language and Unicode. If the toolchain only does A-1, use A-1a. Embed fonts you may legally embed, avoid executable content and A-3 attachments (ship the CSV as a separate attachment), validate every file. (CONARQ OT 4 Rec. 1, 2, 5, 9–13 and annex.)
13. **Make the record verifiable**: print generation time (America/Sao_Paulo, dd/mm/aaaa HH:mm), document number, and a verification line (short hash plus a URL where the sent file's hash can be checked); store the exact sent bytes. Say plainly whether the file is ICP-Brasil signed. Name the file `Resumo diário 0287_2026_Fala Canoas 2026-10-13.pdf`. (SEI verifier/CRC footer pattern; Manual 6.4.2 and 5.2.l.) The hash step is an inference.
14. **The e-mail body stands alone**: specific subject, formal register, per-recipient counts, the public-page link and what the attachment contains, signed "Atenciosamente"; the PDF is the attached record, and an HTML or CSV alternative exists. (Manual 6.4.3–6.4.4; GOV.UK "alongside HTML"; e-MAG 3.8.)
15. **Same template at 1 item and at 200**: a one-item day still gets the cover, index and record block, so staff learn one layout. Optionally add a seven-day count per recipient as a word-sized sparkline on the section strip ("compared to what?"), using real counts only. (Tufte sparklines and EI p.67 [secundário]; inference for use here.)
